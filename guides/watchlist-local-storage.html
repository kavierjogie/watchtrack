How a local-storage watchlist works, and how to keep it safe

Updated 29 October 2026

Some watchlist apps keep your data on their servers behind an account. Others, including WatchTrack, keep it in your browser on your own device. Both work, but they have different trade-offs, and it's worth knowing them before you put years of viewing history into one.

What "stored in your browser" means

Modern browsers give every website its own private storage area. The main one for apps is IndexedDB, a small database inside the browser that can hold structured data and images. Only pages from that same website can read it. Other sites can't, and the website's owner can't either unless the page deliberately uploads it.

The benefits

  • No account: no email, password or profile to create, leak or forget.
  • Private by default: your viewing history isn't sitting in someone else's database.
  • Works offline: the data is already on the device, so updating your progress doesn't need a connection.
  • Fast: reading from the device is quicker than a round trip to a server.

The trade-offs

  • One browser, one copy. Chrome on your laptop, Safari on your phone and Firefox on your desktop each have their own separate storage. There's no automatic sync.
  • Clearing site data deletes it. "Clear browsing data" with cookies and site data selected, or removing a site's data in settings, wipes the library for that site.
  • Private or incognito windows are temporary. Anything saved there is discarded when the window closes.
  • Browsers can clean up storage. When a device runs low on space, browsers may evict data from sites. A site can ask for "persistent" storage to be protected, but the browser decides. Safari can also remove a website's stored data if you haven't used the site for a while; web apps added to the iPhone or iPad Home Screen are treated separately.

How to keep a local watchlist safe

  1. Export a backup regularly, such as after a big batch of changes or once a month. Keep the file somewhere you already back up.
  2. Use the same browser every time on each device, and not a private window.
  3. Check before clearing browsing data. Most browsers let you keep data for specific sites.
  4. On iPhone or iPad, add the app to your Home Screen if you use it often.
  5. Move between devices with a backup file: export on the old one, import on the new one.

How WatchTrack handles this

WatchTrack stores your titles, progress, history, notes and uploaded posters in IndexedDB and asks the browser for persistent storage on first load. Settings shows how much space the library uses and whether it's protected from cleanup. Export backup saves everything as a single JSON file, posters included. Import backup checks the file, previews duplicates, and lets you merge or replace. Searching for titles sends only the search text to the metadata services, never your library.
