How to keep track of the anime and TV episodes you've watched

Updated 29 October 2026

Losing your place in a show is a small problem that happens constantly: you take a break, a new season drops a year later, and you can't remember whether you stopped at episode 7 or 8. A few habits make it go away for good.

Record the next episode, not the last one

"I'm on episode 8" is ambiguous. Did you finish 8, or is 8 the one to start? Writing down the next episode to watch removes the guesswork, and it's the number you actually need when you sit down to watch. If you prefer thinking in "last watched", that's fine too. Just pick one and use it for every show.

Pick the right numbering for each show

Shows are numbered in two different ways, and mixing them up is the most common reason trackers end up wrong.

  • Season + episode (S2 E5) suits most TV and most modern anime, which air in seasons or "cours" with the count starting again at 1. Spy x Family is a typical example.
  • One running number (episode 143) suits long-running anime that are listed as a single sequence, such as Black Clover. Converting these into seasons is confusing because different sites split them differently.

Use whatever numbering the place you watch on uses. That way the number in your tracker matches the number on screen.

Decide what to do with recaps, specials and OVAs

Not every episode moves the story along. Before they trip you up:

  • Recap or filler episodes you skip: note the range (for example 54–56) so your "next episode" jumps over them.
  • Specials, OVAs and movies tied to a series: log them in your history, but don't let them change your position in the main series.
  • Rewatches: track them separately from your first watch, so finishing a rewatch doesn't erase when you originally completed it.

Don't assume another season exists

When you finish a season, it's tempting to set yourself to "Season 3, episode 1". If season 3 hasn't been announced, that entry is a guess, and a year later it's hard to tell a guess from a fact. Mark it as "finished season 2" instead, and move on when the next season is actually listed.

Keep one place as the source of truth

Streaming services remember progress, but only for what you watch on that service, and only until the show leaves it. Keep a single list across every service and every format so you always have the full picture.
